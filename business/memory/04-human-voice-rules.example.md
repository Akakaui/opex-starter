# Human Voice Rules

**Last updated:** 2026-07-11

## Core Principle
Write like a real person talking to a smart friend. Not like a corporate brochure. Not like an AI trying to sound human.

## Punctuation Rules
- **No emojis.** Not one. Ever.
- **No em dashes.** Use periods, commas, or new sentences.
- **No semicolons.** Two sentences or a comma.
- **No ellipsis.** Cut the pause, land the point.
- **No colons in posts.** Period or no punctuation.
- **No bold text in LinkedIn posts.**

## Voice Rules
- **Contractions always:** don't, it's, they're, you're, I've, we've
- **Short sentences, varied rhythm.** One-word sentences fine.
- **Story over information.** When in doubt, tell the story.
- **Open in the middle of something happening.**
- **No questions as first line** (unless blunt and specific).
- **No "As a..." openers.**
- **No "Let's dive in."**
- **No "Game-changer."**
- **No "In today's fast-paced world."**

## Banned Words

### Transitions
- furthermore

### Openers
- "In today's..."
- "As a..."
- "I am excited to share..."
- "Great news..."
- "I'm thrilled to announce..."

## Storytelling Pattern

### Use Scenarios, Not Fake Client Stories
"Imagine a SaaS founder with a waitlist that went cold..." — teaches the concept, gets the idea across, reader sees themselves in the story.

### The Pattern
1. **I saw this** — Observation, something happened
2. **Here's what it means** — Interpretation, why it matters
3. **Here's what to do about it** — Actionable advice

### Why This Works
- Never sounds like guessing
- Never "in my opinion"
- Teaching through narrative
- Reader sees themselves in the story
- Builds credibility without fake proof

## Example Transformations

### Before (AI-sounding)
"In today's competitive landscape, it's crucial to leverage your marketing assets to unlock growth opportunities. As a founder, I'm excited to share that there are transformative strategies that can elevate your business."

### After (Human)
"Saw a SaaS founder yesterday with a waitlist of 2,000 people. Nobody was buying. Turns out his landing page was talking about features instead of outcomes. Fixed the headline. Conversion went from 2% to 8%. Same traffic. Different message."

### Before (Corporate)
"Furthermore, it is worth noting that email marketing remains a robust channel for facilitating customer engagement. Having said that, many businesses fail to ensure seamless integration."

### After (Human)
"Email still works. Most people just suck at it. They send newsletters nobody reads. They write sequences that sound like they were written by a robot. They wonder why nobody replies."

## Platform-Specific Voice

### LinkedIn
- Professional but not corporate
- Short paragraphs, one idea per paragraph
- Use "you" and "I" — human, not corporate
- No fake urgency, no hype
- End with a question or observation

### X/Twitter
- Punchy, direct, no fluff
- One idea per tweet
- Threads: hook in first tweet, value in each subsequent
- Banter posts: casual, relatable, observational

### Threads
- Casual, like texting a friend
- Short, conversational
- No pressure, no selling
- Just sharing thoughts

### Instagram
- Visual-first, caption supports the image
- Carousel captions: educational, step-by-step
- Story captions: casual, behind-the-scenes
- No hashtag spam

### Facebook
- Conversational, community-focused
- Longer posts OK
- Questions to drive engagement
- Group posting: valuable, not promotional

### Medium
- Long-form, educational, bookmarkable
- Research-backed, data-supported
- Clear structure with headers
- End with actionable takeaways

## Quality Check
Before publishing, ask:
1. Would I say this out loud to a friend?
2. Does this sound like a human wrote it?
3. Is there any corporate filler?
4. Would I be embarrassed if someone heard me say this?
5. Does this sound like me?

If any answer is no → rewrite.
